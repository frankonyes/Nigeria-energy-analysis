# Nigeria's Electricity Growth in Context (1990–2014)

A comparative analysis of electricity production and renewable energy adoption in Nigeria versus five peer African economies, using UN International Energy Statistics data.

## Summary

Nigeria's electricity production grew more slowly than comparable economies between 2005 and 2014, and its renewable energy mix remains underdeveloped relative to hydro-rich peers like Kenya and Ghana. This project cleans, analyzes, and visualizes 25 years of UN energy data to quantify that gap and surface where Nigeria's energy sector stands relative to its regional peers.

## Key findings

- **Scale:** In 2014, Nigeria produced ~234,000 GWh of electricity — a mid-tier figure among the peer group, well behind South Africa (~2.0M GWh) and Egypt (~1.3M GWh), but ahead of Ghana (~97,000 GWh) and Kenya (~75,000 GWh).
- **Growth (2005–2014):** Nigeria's electricity production grew just **+32.4%** over the decade — slower than Ghana (+82.1%), Morocco (+70.3%), Kenya (+60.2%), and Egypt (+58.0%). Only South Africa grew slower (+3.4%), but South Africa was already near its production ceiling as the region's dominant producer.
- **Volatility:** Nigeria's year-over-year growth is inconsistent rather than steady — notable declines in 2008 (−7.8%) and 2009 (−5.5%), coinciding with the global financial crisis and known domestic power-sector instability, followed by a sharp recovery in 2010 (+27.7%).
- **Renewable share (2014):** Renewables (hydro, solar, wind, geothermal) made up only **17.6%** of Nigeria's electricity-by-source mix — behind Kenya (71.6%) and Ghana (63.8%), both hydro-heavy, though ahead of Morocco (14.7%), Egypt (8.8%), and coal-dependent South Africa (4.1%).

## Data source

[UN International Energy Statistics](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/unitednations/international-energy-statistics) (Kaggle), sourced from the UN Statistics Division's Energy Statistics Database. Countries compared: Nigeria, South Africa, Egypt, Ghana, Kenya, Morocco.

## Methodology

1. **Data cleaning (Python/pandas):** Filtered the raw dataset (1.2M rows) to the six countries and relevant commodities. Fixed inconsistent commodity naming (typos, casing) and split the combined `commodity_transaction` field into separate `commodity` and `transaction` columns.

2. **Metric classification:** Tagged each row as `Production`, `Capacity`, `Reserves`, or `Sub-breakdown` to avoid a data integrity error that would have otherwise occurred — the raw data mixes energy-flow metrics (kilowatt-**hours**, a production measure) with power-capacity metrics (kilowatts, a capacity measure) under the same commodity name. Rows were classified by unit, not name alone, so production totals are never contaminated by capacity or reserve figures.

3. **SQL analysis:** Loaded the cleaned data into a relational database and wrote queries using `GROUP BY`/`SUM` aggregation, a self-join for year-over-year growth comparison, `CASE WHEN` conditional aggregation for renewable classification, and a `LAG()` window function for year-over-year volatility — each cross-validated against the Python results to confirm correctness.

4. **Visualization (Power BI):** Built a four-panel dashboard — production trend over time, 2014 country ranking, renewable share by country, and Nigeria's year-over-year volatility — using DAX measures for `Total Production (GWh)` and `Renewable Share %`.

## Tools used

- **Python** (pandas) — data cleaning, exploratory analysis
- **SQL** (SQLite) — aggregation, self-joins, conditional logic, window functions
- **Power BI** — dashboard and DAX measures

## Caveats

- Data coverage runs 1990–2014 for all six countries; more recent trends are not reflected.
- "Renewable share" is calculated only from rows with electricity-by-source detail (hydro, solar, wind, geothermal, and non-renewable sources broken out individually) — it excludes any source not itemized in the original dataset.
- Figures are drawn directly from UN-reported statistics and have not been cross-checked against national energy ministry data.
